Tectonic Earth

An interactive reconstruction of Earth's surface across 1.25 billion years — from the supercontinent Rodinia through Pangaea to the projected future supercontinent Pangaea Proxima. Land, mountains, shelves and inland seas come from published paleo-elevation data at 5-million-year steps.

How it animates

The page doesn't ship pictures of each era — it ships elevation and rainfall fields, and builds the world in a shader as you scrub. Because it interpolates the height field, a coastline genuinely migrates between keyframes instead of one image dissolving into another, so motion stays smooth at any playback speed. Relief is shaded per pixel, so terrain keeps its bite at any zoom. The future is the present-day map carried by rigid plate rotations, which is why it holds the same detail as today and leaves no seam at the present.

Layers through time

The present-day layers are surveyed data only for today. Going back, plate motion is measured from the reconstruction itself — consecutive keyframes are the same crust a few million years apart, so matching one against the next recovers how fast and which way each patch of surface travelled. Boundaries follow from that same field: crust pulling apart is a ridge, crust converging a trench. The two layers therefore always agree. Where there is no structure to match — bare abyssal plain — no motion is claimed rather than invented. Volcanism carries age windows, but a flood basalt is an eruption for a moment and a landform for far longer, so each province stays on the map for as long as it stood as high ground — the Deccan still holds up the Western Ghats, while CAMP, the largest of them all, was buried in its own rift basins almost as it erupted and is a landform essentially nowhere. Plumes are anchored in the mantle while plates slide over them, so those markers stay put and the crust moves past; provinces, which ride away on the plate, are carried back to the crust they erupted through.

Names and life

A feature that lasts 300 million years is not doing the same thing throughout, so the long-lived ones carry a description per phase of their life: the Appalachians read as a rising arc collision at 450 Ma, a Himalayan-scale range at 290, and a worn root today. Names are laid out rather than merely projected — each takes the nearest free slot to its feature, with a leader line when it has to move, and is dropped rather than drawn on top of another. Clicking a landmass also shows what its rocks preserve at that moment, and the life of the age is drawn alongside.

How the climate is derived

Biomes aren't painted on by latitude. Each frame runs a small climate model: prevailing winds (tropical easterlies, mid-latitude westerlies) carry moisture inland from the sea, drying as they go, so continental interiors go arid on their own — which is why Pangaea grows a desert heart while its coasts and Tethys shores stay monsoon-green. Air forced over mountains rains out and leaves a rain shadow behind them (the Atacama and Patagonia fall out of the model, not out of a lookup table). Vegetation is then set by rainfall against evaporative demand, so cold, dry Siberia is taiga while a subtropical plain on the same rainfall is desert.

Data sources

  • Paleogeography & elevation — Scotese & Wright (2018), PALEOMAP PaleoDEMs for the Phanerozoic (0–540 Ma), 6-arc-minute grids. CC-BY 4.0.
  • Present plate motions — NNR-MORVEL56 (Argus, Gordon & DeMets, 2011): real Euler poles drive the motion-vector field.
  • Plate boundaries — Bird (2003), PB2002 — classified as ridges, trenches and transform faults.
  • Climate & biosphere — a literature-informed model (Scotese climate zones, Haq/Hallam sea-level curves, Late-Paleozoic and Neoproterozoic glaciations). Greenhouse/icehouse anomalies are applied with polar amplification, so hothouse worlds gain ice-free poles while the tropics barely move.
  • Volcanism & impacts — the IUGS Large Igneous Provinces Commission record and Ernst's LIP compilation, the Earth Impact Database and Schmieder & Kring (2020), with ages taken from the primary geochronology where it has been revised.
  • Intervals — boundary ages follow the ICS chart v2024/12. Several differ from figures still in wide circulation: base Ordovician is 486.85 Ma, base Cretaceous 143.1 Ma.
  • Life & biomes — biome names are chosen to suit their period rather than the present: there is no grassland before the Cenozoic, and before land plants the terrestrial world is microbial crust and bare regolith.
  • Illustrations — most are silhouettes from PhyloPic, traced by their contributors from skeletal reconstructions and life restorations, used under CC0, Public Domain Mark or CC-BY and credited individually in the About page. A silhouette is one artist's reading of an incomplete fossil, not a photograph. Where PhyloPic has nothing to trace — an assemblage like a reef, a sedimentary structure like a stromatolite — the drawing is schematic.

Pre-540 Ma and future frames are authored reconstructions (real cratons rotated into supercontinent fits) and are illustrative rather than exact. Deep-time positions carry real uncertainty; treat this as a visualization of the published record, not a precise map.

The timeline

Rodinia to the next supercontinent

Moving through the timeline changes the whole configuration: Rodinia and a single world ocean at 1000 Ma, the Cryogenian glaciations, Pangaea's arid interior, the opening of the Atlantic, and — beyond the present — the assembly of a future supercontinent under a brightening Sun. Climate, sea level, ice, CO2 and O2 follow published estimates for each interval. The deep past and deep future are the least constrained; the future is shown as one plausible scenario, with the biosphere contracting to refugia rather than disappearing.

What you can see

A whole Earth system, modeled

Real plate motion

Ridges, trenches and transforms from the Merdith 2021 rotation model — moving continuously, keeping their names, ocean floor as active as the continents.

Climate & biomes

Winds carry moisture inland and dry the interiors; mountains cast rain shadows; vegetation follows rainfall against evaporation — so Pangaea grows a desert heart on its own.

Photoreal terrain

Per-pixel relief and hillshade, slope-bare rock on mountain flanks, ocean bathymetry, and glaciers from a snowline that answers to both cold and snowfall.

Rivers, marshes & deltas

Modeled drainage read from the lows of the reconstructed terrain and weighted by rainfall — channels in the valleys, marshes in wet flats, deltas at wet coasts.

Sky & sea

A cloud layer driven by each era's own rainfall (tropical convergence, storm tracks, clear desert belts), a day/night terminator, limb haze, and era-appropriate ocean colour.

Deep-time events

73 confirmed impact structures and 37 large igneous provinces, each carried along its plate toward where that crust sat at the time (an estimate that recovers direction better than distance).

The fossil record

Characteristic life per region and interval — flora and fauna drawn from the record, with hand-made illustrations — plus named seas, deserts, plateaus and lakes.

The future

Solar brightening and the next supercontinent — the most uncertain stretch of the timeline, shown as one plausible scenario with the land biosphere thinning toward refugia.

Method

Drainage, marshes, and deltas

Individual ancient river courses are not known, so none are drawn from a map. Drainage is instead derived from data the model already carries: the topographic lows of the reconstructed elevation field, weighted by local rainfall. Narrow incised lows are rendered as river channels, broad wet flats as marsh, and the same at a coastline as a delta plain. The resolution is valley-scale (tens of kilometres), so the result represents major drainage rather than individual tributaries, and it becomes less certain in deeper time as the underlying topography does.

As a check, the same method applied to the present-day elevation model places channels in the real Himalayan valleys draining to the Bay of Bengal. These are modeled drainage patterns, not mapped rivers.

Under the hood

How it's built

The engine

Ships elevation, rainfall and motion textures per 5-Myr keyframe; a GLSL shader interpolates them and recomputes temperature, relief, biomes, water and sky per pixel — nothing is a pre-baked picture.

Plate motion

The Merdith et al. (2021) full-plate rotation model, resolved with pyGPlates into continuously-closing plate topologies and classified boundaries.

Paleogeography

Scotese & Wright PALEOMAP paleo-elevation models for 0–540 Ma; an authored craton composite deeper in the Precambrian; the present terrain rotated forward for the future.

Climate

Temperature from latitude, elevation and an era anomaly; rainfall from wind-driven moisture transport, orographic rain shadow and Budyko humidity — calibrated to published GMST, CO2 and O2 curves.

Sun & future

Solar luminosity from the Gough (1981) standard model (−8% at 1000 Ma, brighter ahead); the deep future calibrated to Farnsworth et al. (2024) on the same Pangaea Ultima reconstruction.

Events & life

Impact structures vetted against the Impact Earth database and Schmieder & Kring (2020); fossil-record biota per region and interval, each event and label written up by hand.
